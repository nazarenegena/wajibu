# Project Wajibu — Demo Video Script

**Target length:** 90 seconds
**Format:** Screen recording with voiceover. Optional 3-second talking-head intro.
**Aspect ratio:** 16:9, 1080p, 30fps

---

## 0:00–0:08 — Hook

**Visual:** A real Nyeri tender PDF on screen. Slowly scroll past dense legal text. Highlight two or three jargon terms in red as you pass them.

**Voiceover:**
> "In Nyeri, tenders are public. But for most people, they might as well be written in another language."

---

## 0:08–0:20 — Problem

**Visual:** Keep scrolling the PDF. Pause on a buried deadline on page 14. Zoom slightly.

**Voiceover:**
> "Technical English. Procurement jargon. Deadlines buried on page fourteen. If you can't understand it, you can't bid. You can't question it. You're shut out."

---

## 0:20–0:32 — Solution intro

**Visual:** Cut to the Wajibu landing page. Cursor moves to "Analyse a document."

**Voiceover:**
> "This is Project Wajibu. It takes a real county tender and explains it in plain Swahili or English — in seconds. And it shows you exactly where every claim comes from."

---

## 0:32–1:05 — Live demo (no cuts)

**Visual:** One continuous screen recording. Follow these steps in order:

1. Select the **NYEWASCO tender** from the sample cards
2. Plain-language **summary appears** in English
3. Click **"Show original text"** — the source passage expands beneath the summary
4. Toggle to **Swahili** — the whole page switches language
5. Tap **"Framework Contract"** in the jargon buster — plain meaning pops up
6. Scroll to the **red flag** — short deadline, with the source quote shown
7. Click **"Text-only mode"** — visuals strip away, layout simplifies
8. Open the **SMS preview** — show the ≤160-character summary and the SMS chat mock

**Voiceover:**
> "Every claim cites the source. Every technical term is explained. Every red flag points back to the exact line in the document. And for slow connections or feature phones — a text-only mode, and an SMS summary that fits in a hundred and sixty characters."

---

## 1:05–1:20 — Accessibility & next steps

**Visual:** Scroll back up. Click the **text-size toggle** — text grows. Then scroll to the **Next Steps** card.

**Voiceover:**
> "Text can be enlarged without browser zoom. And every result ends with a clear next step: the question to ask, and who to ask. Because understanding is only useful if it leads to action."

---

## 1:20–1:30 — Close

**Visual:** Wajibu logo + tagline, centred. Fade out.

**Voiceover:**
> "Project Wajibu. Because public money should be publicly understood."

## Recording Checklist

**Before recording:**
- [ ] Live app is deployed and working (not localhost)
- [ ] Sample documents pre-loaded so there's no upload wait
- [ ] Browser in a clean window — no bookmarks bar, no extra tabs, no notifications
- [ ] System theme set to whichever mode you want to demo (dark is more striking)
- [ ] Font size set to "base" before recording
- [ ] Text-only mode OFF before recording
- [ ] Zoom level at 100% so text is readable at 1080p
- [ ] Cursor visible and slow — don't flick between elements

**While recording:**
- [ ] Speak slowly. The screen is doing the work.
- [ ] Pause briefly between steps — 0.5 seconds is enough
- [ ] Don't say "um" or narrate every click. Let the app breathe.
- [ ] If you fumble a step, restart. Don't patch in editing.

**After recording:**
- [ ] Trim the first and last frames cleanly
- [ ] Verify total length is under 1:30
- [ ] Export at 1080p, H.264, under 100MB
- [ ] Upload to YouTube (unlisted) or Vimeo
- [ ] Paste the link into README.md and SUMMARY.md

---

## Fallback if a feature breaks during recording

Do not stop and debug on camera. Skip the broken feature and continue. You can re-record after. A clean 85-second video is better than a janky 90-second one.

---

## Optional B-roll (if you have time)

- 3 seconds of a real Nyeri street or market (shot on your phone)
- 2 seconds of someone reading a notice on a public wall
- 2 seconds of hands holding a basic feature phone

Use sparingly. No B-roll is better than distracting B-roll. The app is the story.
